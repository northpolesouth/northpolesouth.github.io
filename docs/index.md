# North Pole South

This year marks our 20th year of using Light-O-Rama to run our Christmas light show in Apache, Oklahoma. I remember the very first sequence I created from scratch: *Believe*, by Josh Groban. It took FOREVER! I spent every free minute for a week on it.

Technology has changed drastically since then. When we started, LED Christmas lights were a rare, pricey novelty, and smart pixels didn't exist. The only thing we knew about AI was the Will Smith movie *I, Robot*.

Twenty years later, it's a different landscape. Let me stress: I'm not a software engineer. I'm a Director of User Assistance Development at a major software company. In the olden days we called that technical writing. I've spent the past two years learning about AI and helping my team bring it into their daily work, so it seemed only natural to do the same to get the lights up and running this year.

I'll be honest: some years, getting the show ready has been such a chore that I almost dreaded putting it on. This year, AI took on the tedious parts, and most of the stress went with them. The show is bigger and better than it's ever been, and I'm enjoying it again.

I know AI is a polarizing topic. I'm not suggesting it should replace the craft of sequencing. For me it's a tool: it handles the grunt work, and I still make the decisions, check every result, and decide what goes into the show.

These pages capture what I did, so anyone feeling adventurous can try it for themselves. (Fittingly, *Believe* was also the first song my new AI process lip-synced from start to finish.)

This is a personal hobby project, not connected to my employer.
